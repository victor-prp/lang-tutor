# In-messenger translation and writing-help tools (bots, keyboards, overlays, built-in messenger and OS features), as of October 2026

Scope: tools that work *inside* WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, Messenger, Signal, Viber, LINE and WeChat to translate received messages or help write outgoing ones, and how close each comes to a language-*learning* product (tap a word, save vocabulary, explain grammar) as opposed to pure translation. Research date: 2026-10-19. Hebrew (founder's L1) and Italian (target language) support is flagged wherever a source states it.

## Native messenger and phone-OS features: what each does, per-message vs word-level, grammar correction

### Takeaway
By October 2026 every major messenger and phone OS translates received messages, either per message (long-press, then Translate) or for a whole chat (auto-translate). **None of them lets the user tap a single word in a received message for its translation, and none saves vocabulary.** Writing help (fix grammar, change tone, translate before sending) now ships in Telegram (AI Editor, March 2026), WhatsApp (Writing Help, English only at launch), Samsung (Writing assist) and Apple (Writing Tools). Of these, only Apple's Proofread is documented as giving **an explanation for each change**. Italian is widely supported. **Hebrew is missing from every on-device native translator whose language list I could find** (WhatsApp on both platforms, Apple, Samsung, Pixel).

### Cited Findings

More were promised with no timeline. — [About Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/introducing-message-translations-whatsapp/); [9to5Google: auto-translate of a whole chat was Android-only at launch](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/23/whatsapp-message-translations-auto-translation-support-exclusive-to-android/)
- **Beta, March 2026:** WhatsApp beta for Android 2.26.12.4 added 13 language packs: Czech, German, French, Indonesian, **Italian**, Latvian, Dutch, Polish, Swedish, Turkish, Ukrainian, Urdu and simplified Mandarin, for 19 packs in total. Hebrew is not on the list. — [WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-beta-for-android-adds-13-language-packs-for-message-translations/)
- **In development, March 2026, not confirmed shipped:** iOS beta 26.11.10.70 contains a per-chat option to automatically translate incoming messages across up to 21 languages, using Apple's on-device packs. - **WhatsApp "Writing Help" (Aug 2025):** an AI assistant that can rephrase or proofread a draft or change its tone (Professional, Funny, Supportive, Proofread). It runs on Meta's "Private Processing", and Meta says neither WhatsApp nor Meta can read the original text or the rewrites. **Telegram: per-message translation (free), whole-chat translation (Premium), AI Editor (March 2026)**
- API docs: `messages.translateText` translates given messages. Its only Premium gate is that bold, italic and link formatting is preserved for Premium users only. "Telegram Premium users may even enable real-time chat translation": the client runs a **local language-recognition model** on incoming messages. It offers the auto-translate popup only after ≥8 messages have been processed, a language was detected in ≥35% of them, and ≥65% of those are foreign. **Channels** that reach a boost level can turn on autotranslation for *all* their users, Premium or not. The docs do not describe translating parts of a message or individual words. — [Telegram API: Message translation](https://core.telegram.org/api/translation)
- Whole-chat translation for Premium users arrived in early 2023. Single-message translation stayed free. — [9to5Mac, Feb 2023 (older)](https://9to5mac.com/2023/02/04/telegram-update-translation-annual-premium/); [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/how-to-translate-messages-in-telegram/)
- **AI Editor (31 Mar 2026):** "The message bar now has a built-in editor that can instantly fix grammar, rewrite your text in a different style or translate your message into another language." The styles are Formal, Short, Tribal, Corp, Zen, Biblical and Viking, and translation can be combined with a style. It is triggered by typing more than 3 lines and tapping the "Ai" icon above Send. It is powered by Telegram's **Cocoon** network, with requests "processed in a confidential environment with zero access to user data". The post does not say whether it is free or Premium, and it does not say whether grammar fixes are explained. — [Telegram blog, 31 Mar 2026](https://telegram.org/blog/ai-editor-mighty-polls-and-more)
- The Telegram FAQ says that "Telegram's AI features like translation and custom tones are powered by Cocoon" and that they do not send data to third parties. — [Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq)
- Sources disagree on the launch date: 31 Mar 2026 by [Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/news/telegram-ai-editor-bot-management-march-2026-update) and the Telegram blog, versus the "April 2026 update" (v12.6) by [Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/04/01/telegram-12-6-update-for-android-ios/).

**Apple: Live Translation in Messages (iOS 26, shipped Sep 2025) and Writing Tools**
- Live Translation in Messages supports English (US, UK), French (France), German, **Italian**, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish (Spain) and Chinese (simplified). **No Hebrew.** — [Apple Newsroom, Sep 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/new-apple-intelligence-features-are-available-today/)
- How it works: the user turns on "Automatically Translate" per contact and picks a "Translate From" language, which can mean a ~900 MB language pack. Outgoing messages show the user's text with the translation. Incoming messages show the original with the translation underneath. A "Translating [language]" tab switches views. It needs an Apple Intelligence device (iPhone 15 Pro, 16, Air or 17). If the other person is on an older iPhone or on Android, their incoming messages are still translated, but the user's replies are not translated for them. The article mentions no word-level tap and no grammar correction. It is about Apple's Messages app only. — [MacRumors, 24 Sep 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-live-translation-in-messages/)
- **Writing Tools → Proofread** (Apple Intelligence) checks spelling, grammar, word choice and sentence structure. Changes are underlined, and the user can step through each one "to view changes and an explanation for each change". Writing Tools work "in most places you write, including third-party apps and websites", which would include WhatsApp's or Telegram's text field. Italian is a listed Apple Intelligence language. — [Apple Support: Writing Tools on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-the-right-words-with-writing-tools-iph6f08da1d2/ios)

### Inferences
- **Word-level tap is open ground.** Every native feature translates a whole message or a whole chat. None of the sources describes tapping one word of a received message for its meaning, part of speech or lemma, or saving it. This is the clearest gap relative to the proposed product.
- **Translation is free and built in.** WhatsApp (on-device), Apple (on-device), Pixel (on-device), Samsung (free basic tier), Telegram (free per message) and Viber all ship translation at no cost. A product whose value is "translate my chats" competes with the OS and the messenger itself. The defensible part is the *learning layer*: words, explanations, review.
- **"Correct before sending" is commoditising, but without teaching.** Telegram AI Editor, WhatsApp Writing Help, Samsung Writing assist and Gboard Proofread all *fix* a draft. Only Apple's Proofread is documented as explaining each change, and none of them tracks a user's recurring mistakes. Explaining *why* a correction was made, and remembering it, is the gap.
- **Hebrew is the weak spot of on-device translation.** No Hebrew appears in the WhatsApp Android packs (as of the March 2026 beta), Apple's on-device list, Apple Live Translation, Samsung Writing assist or Pixel chat Live Translate. Server-side translators (Telegram, Viber via Google Translate, Gboard) probably do cover Hebrew, since Google Translate does, but none of the sources found states this. For a Hebrew-speaking learner of Italian, the built-in WhatsApp and iMessage translators probably cannot do Hebrew↔Italian today. Translating Italian into English works.
- **Italian is well covered** by WhatsApp (Android beta, iPhone via Apple), Apple Live Translation, Samsung, Pixel and (beta) Grammarly, so Italian reading help is the most crowded slice of the market.

### Gaps
- I did not reach WhatsApp's own help-centre article on translation (the FAQ URL tried returned "not found"). I found no confirmation that the 13 Android packs from the March 2026 beta reached stable, or that iOS auto-translate shipped.
- I found no current (2026) language list for WhatsApp Writing Help beyond "English at launch", and no list for the Telegram AI Editor. Whether the Telegram AI Editor is free or Premium is not stated in the official post.
- I found no official statement on whether Telegram or Viber translation supports Hebrew. It probably does, because Viber names Google Translate, but this is unverified.
- Google Messages translation: no official source found. Neither Circle to Search's "translate screen" feature, Google Lens, nor Samsung Bixby Vision was researched in depth.
- No source says whether Apple's Live Translation in Messages lets the user long-press a single word, as opposed to a whole message.
- Whether Hebrew is supported by Apple Intelligence Writing Tools could not be confirmed. I found no listing that includes it.

## Keyboards: Gboard, SwiftKey, Grammarly, LanguageTool, Samsung Keyboard, "learning keyboards". Do they explain corrections or just fix them?

### Takeaway
Keyboards are the one route that works in *every* messenger, and the big ones all translate what you type (Gboard, SwiftKey) or fix it (Gboard Proofread, Samsung Writing assist). They mostly **fix without explaining**. Grammarly's keyboard is the exception: it shows an explanation for each suggestion, but its keyboard autocorrect is English-only and its other languages are beta. I found **no maintained keyboard built for language learning** that teaches vocabulary or explains grammar while you chat. The examples found are a defunct-looking research or startup project (WordsU) and typing tutors that are not system keyboards.

### Cited Findings
- **Gboard translate mode:** on Android, an integrated Google Translate mode translates what you type into another language on the fly, from a bar above the keyboard. — [Android Ayuda](https://en.androidayuda.com/gboard-keyboard-google-integrates-translator/). The iOS version got translation in 2019, covering all Google Translate languages. — [GSMArena (2019, older)](https://m.gsmarena.com/gboard_for_ios_now_has_inapp_translation_capabilities_-news-36048.php)
- Using Gboard to read a received WhatsApp message is manual: the user copies it, opens Gboard's translate option and pastes. — [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/how-to-translate-whatsapp-messages/)
- **Gboard Proofread** checks typed, pasted or dictated text "for writing errors like typos, grammar, and punctuation". "Fix it" applies the suggestions and Undo reverts them. The official page lists Pixel 8/8 Pro, English only, US. It gives no per-error explanation. — [Gboard Help](https://support.google.com/gboard/answer/14164531?hl=en)
- Conflict on privacy: guides say the Gemini Nano tools run on-device, but the beta prompt reported by 9to5Google said "The text that's proofread will be sent to Google and temporarily processed". — [Morung Express (9to5Google-based)](https://morungexpress.com/google-adds-ai-powered-proofread-feature-in-gboard)
- **Microsoft SwiftKey:** Microsoft Translator was added to the toolbar in 2018, translating in 60+ languages. This source is older, and I did not verify the 2026 state. — [Droid Life, Sep 2018](https://www.droid-life.com/2018/09/27/swiftkey-adds-microsoft-translator-for-easy-translations/)
- **Grammarly keyboard:** tapping the Grammarly button "expands the errors with explanations for the proposed change", and correction bubbles appear above the keyboard. — [Android Police, Dec 2017 (older)](https://www.androidpolice.com/2017/12/14/grammarly-releases-android-keyboard-make-grammar-better/)
- Grammarly added beta writing help for Spanish, French, German, Portuguese and **Italian** in Sep 2025, plus translation across 19 languages. — [Business Wire, 10 Sep 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250910688436/en/Grammarly-Expands-Beyond-English-With-AI-Writing-Assistance-in-5-New-Languages). The translation surfaces named were Windows, Mac, Chrome and Figma, not the mobile keyboard. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/grammarly-goes-multilingual-adds-support-for-french-german-spanish-italian-and-portuguese-493554-2025-09-11)
- The iOS App Store listing says the Grammarly keyboard's autocorrect and predictions are English only, while its rewrites and suggestions cover multiple languages. — [App Store: Grammarly](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1158877342)
- A March 2026 release note mentions 17 more beta languages (Turkish, Polish, Dutch, Czech, Vietnamese, Hungarian, among others). I saw only a truncated snippet and could not confirm whether Hebrew is included. — [releases.sh](https://releases.sh/superhuman/grammarly.md)
- **LanguageTool:** I found no official mobile keyboard. A forum thread asking for one got no official answer. — [LanguageTool Forum](https://forum.languagetool.org/t/languagetool-on-android/11647). It ships as a Firefox-for-Android add-on. — [Mozilla Add-ons](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/android/addon/languagetool)
- **Samsung Keyboard:** Writing assist gives spelling and grammar correction (Replace/Copy) and styles, with no documented explanations. See the native section. — [Samsung US](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10000943/)
- **Learning keyboards:** *WordsU* was an alternate keyboard whose autocorrect tried to expand the user's vocabulary, aimed at ESL and SAT learners. Its current availability is unconfirmed. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/smartphone-keyboard-teaches-while-you-text). *Lingotype* is a typing-based language app with translations, audio and spaced repetition, but it is **not** a system keyboard. — [Lingotype](https://www.lingotype.app/). *ThaiKey* teaches typing Thai, with 53 lessons. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/thaikey-learn-thai-keyboard/id6456892063?l=ar). *CleverType* markets AI keyboards that suggest vocabulary and adapt to the user's level, with unverified claims. — [CleverType](https://www.clevertype.co/post/multilingual-typing-made-easy-ai-keyboards-for-every-language)

### Inferences
- A keyboard is the only route that reaches *outgoing* text in every messenger, including WhatsApp and Signal, without an official API. That makes "help me write my sentence correctly before sending" technically feasible as a custom keyboard (Android InputMethodService, iOS keyboard extension). The incumbents occupy that space but leave the "explain and teach" layer empty.
- A keyboard cannot read *received* messages, so a keyboard alone cannot deliver "tap a word in the message I received". That half would need an overlay or accessibility service, the OS share sheet, a desktop extension, or a bot the user forwards messages to.

### Gaps
- I did not verify SwiftKey's 2026 translation or Copilot rewrite features (the only source found is from 2018).
- I found no 2026 list of languages supported by Gboard Proofread or Gboard's Writing Tools beyond the official "English, US".
- I found no evidence that any mainstream keyboard (Gboard, SwiftKey, Samsung) offers save-to-vocabulary or spaced repetition.

## Bots: Telegram translation and tutor bots, WhatsApp-number AI tutors. Products, user counts, pricing

### Takeaway
There is a crowded long tail of **AI tutor bots on WhatsApp and Telegram** that correct the user's messages and often explain mistakes (Parlai, Polyato, LangBuddy, AudioLingo, Poly, Telearn, Julia). They are all **1:1 conversations with the bot**, not help inside the user's chats with real people. Pricing is freemium, around €12/month at the top tier. Public user counts are small or unstated. Translation bots that sit inside group chats exist (LINE AutoTranslate). None of the products found combines "help me in my real chat with a friend" with learning features.

### Cited Findings
- **Parlai (WhatsApp; Vienna startup):** "Receive real-time corrections on grammar and vocabulary". It has voice messages and calls, and Premium adds "Corrections and pronunciation feedback" and vocabulary quizzes. It lists 31 languages, including **Hebrew** and **Italian**. Free is €0 with "Limited practice per day". Premium is €12/month with unlimited practice and calls of up to 30 minutes. It claims "10k learners". — [Parlai](https://www.parlai.app/)
- Conflict: Chatimize says Parlai supports 16 languages and that calls were not yet available. — [Chatimize](https://chatimize.com/whatsapp-chatbot-examples/)
- **Polyato (WhatsApp):** "Gentle corrections right when you need them", voice notes back and forth, games, reading and grammar practice inside WhatsApp, and Polly is "ready to explain tricky stuff". It claims 84 languages, including **Hebrew** and **Italian**, with explanations available in the user's own language. Basic is free. Premium (no price shown) adds "Unlimited daily conversations". It states no user count. — [Polyato](https://www.polyato.com/)
- **LangBuddy:** it corrects errors during the chat with a one-line reason and claims 300+ languages. — [LangBuddy](https://langbuddy.ai/)
- **AudioLingo:** an English-only WhatsApp chatbot that "gently corrects you and explains why". — [AudioLingo](https://www.audiolingo.app/tools/whatsapp-chatbot-learn-english/)
- **Poly (Telegram):** direct corrections after each turn plus subtle recasts, in 17 languages. — [Poly / bot-polyglott](https://bot-polyglott.net/en/). **Telearn (Telegram):** English speaking practice with "instant grammar feedback" and IELTS drills. — [Telearn](https://telearn.net/). **Julia (Telegram):** a ChatGPT-powered English bot with a B1 course and real-time voice. — [Julia](https://enjulia.com/)
- Open-source Telegram tutor bots follow the same pattern (an LLM behind aiogram or n8n) and offer corrections in the user's native language, mistake tracking and daily limits. — [GitHub: english-tutor-bot](https://github.com/melkam59/english-tutor-bot); [Medium tutorial](https://medium.com/philips-technology-blog/build-your-own-ai-english-tutor-telegram-bot-local-free-d739621e7b1c)
- A developer write-up on WhatsApp tutors suggests a pattern of replying with a small correction, asking whether the learner wants the reason, and explaining on a "why" reply. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/elenarevicheva/building-ai-language-tutors-on-whatsapp-why-messaging-apps-beat-web-11ke)
- **Translation bot inside group chats:** LINE AutoTranslate auto-detects each message's language and translates it into the configured target languages (33 languages; 2 targets free, 4 Premium). — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/line-autotranslate)

### Inferences
- The tutor-bot category proves that "learn by chatting inside WhatsApp or Telegram" is a familiar, monetisable pattern (free tier, ~€12/month Premium). But it puts the learner in a chat with a **bot**, not a person. The proposed product's distinguishing claim, helping in real conversations with real people, is not what these bots do.
- With a bot, the user has to forward or copy a friend's message to it to get help. Nothing found automates that inside the original chat, apart from LINE-style group bots that the whole group must accept.
- The "correct briefly, explain on request" pattern is already a documented convention among builders, so it would not differentiate.

### Gaps
- I found no independently verified user counts for any WhatsApp or Telegram tutor bot. Parlai's "10k learners" is self-reported, and its live counters showed 0.
- I found no Telegram *translation* bot with public usage numbers (for example inline translator bots). The search returned mostly tutor bots and GitHub projects.
- Polyato's Premium price and company identity are not stated. LangBuddy's pricing was not retrieved.
- An attempt to check "Lingogram: TG Client with AI", a third-party Telegram client on the App Store, returned 404, so its features are unknown.

## Overlay / accessibility-service apps and browser extensions that translate any on-screen chat. Word-level tap and save-to-vocabulary?

### Takeaway
On Android, floating-bubble apps that use the Accessibility Service and screen capture translate text inside WhatsApp, Telegram and LINE without leaving the chat. One large app, **Instant Translate On Screen (5M+ installs)**, already adds a learner layer: a personal wordbook and a "Word Mode" with spaced review, flashcards and quizzes. It is still drag-a-bubble-over-text, not tap a word inside the message. On desktop, WhatsApp Web extensions (DeepL, Flow Translate) translate per message, and none of those found saves words.

### Cited Findings
- **Instant Translate On Screen (Android):** users can "save tricky vocabulary into Word Mode for spaced review, flashcards, and quizzes". It has a "personal wordbook" with pronunciation and translation history and is "Great for WhatsApp, LINE, Telegram, Instagram DM, and Discord conversations". It "requires screen capture and accessibility permissions to read text from your screen". A floating "Magic Dot" bubble is dragged over text and helps the user "reply in any input box". It claims 200+ languages, 5M+ installs, 73K+ Google Play reviews and a 4.5★ rating. The page describes no single-word tap (the user drags the bubble over a region). Pricing is not stated. — [Instant Translate On Screen site](https://screen-translate.vercel.app/); [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.spaceship.screen.textcopy&hl=en)
- **Tap To Translate Screen (EZ Screen Translator):** one-tap translation of on-screen text for chats, comics and games. Its chat mode uses the Accessibility Service to **fill the text box** with the translated reply. Its listing shows no flashcards. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tap_to_translate.snap_translate&hl=en_US)
- **Hi Translate – Chat translator (TexAI):** a floating icon dragged over apps, including WhatsApp, translates on-screen text. The developer says Accessibility APIs are used only to read on-screen text. The developer's "500M+ users" figure is promotional and unverified. — [Cafebazaar listing](https://cafebazaar.ir/app/com.zaz.translate?l=en); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744311749)
- **Google Translate "Tap to Translate" (Android):** it floats over chats and translates any message the user copies. — [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/how-to-translate-whatsapp-messages/)
- **Google Translate "Practice" (beta, Aug 2025):** a Gemini-powered learning mode in which users "listen to conversations and tap the words you hear" or practise speaking, in scenarios tailored to level and goals. At launch it covered English speakers learning Spanish and French, and Spanish, French and Portuguese speakers learning English. It was free at launch. It lives inside the Translate app, not inside messengers, and has **no Hebrew or Italian** at launch. — [TechCrunch, 26 Aug 2025](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/08/26/google-translate-takes-on-duolingo-with-new-language-learning-tools/)
- **Vocably:** a select → translate → save → learn flow via the Android share sheet, with flashcards and quizzes. It has no chat overlay. — [Vocably](https://vocably.pro/)
- **WhatsApp Web extensions:** *DeepL* integrates with WhatsApp Web in its Chrome, Edge and Firefox extensions, but not in the mobile app. — [DeepL Support](https://support.deepl.com/hc/en-us/articles/7786508619420-DeepL-for-WhatsApp-Web). *Flow Translate for WhatsApp Web* uses Chrome's built-in on-device translation, translates a message only when its button is pressed, and supports 39 languages. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/flow-translate-for-whatsa/mngnfieandhlpbbedebnokeghkfjpgkc). *WhatsApp Web Translator* claims 100+ languages and local translation. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/whatsapp-web-translator-r/ohijjchbbjmgefelmjjphibcgmnlmhfl). None of these listings mentions saving words.

### Inferences
- The Android accessibility-overlay route is proven at scale (millions of installs) and is the only mobile route found that can both *read received messages* and *write into the input box* of WhatsApp or Telegram. It is therefore the closest technical analogue to the proposed product on Android. On iOS no equivalent exists in any source found: every iOS approach found depends on copying text or on the OS's own features.
- Instant Translate On Screen is the nearest existing competitor to "tap a word and save it from a chat". Its interaction is region capture with a bubble rather than tapping a word in place, and it explains no grammar for outgoing messages. A product doing true word-level tap and save, plus explained corrections before sending, would still be differentiated, but it should be benchmarked against this app.
- Accessibility-service apps carry permission friction and Play Store policy risk, which the sources hint at by noting "broad device permissions". The policy implications were not researched here.

### Gaps
- Google Lens or Circle to Search "translate screen", and Samsung Bixby Vision, were not researched. I have no sources on whether they allow tapping a single word in a chat.
- The Google Play listing for Instant Translate On Screen could not be fetched directly. Install and rating figures come from the developer's site and search snippets of the listing. Its pricing (subscription or in-app purchases) and Hebrew support are unknown, beyond "200+ languages".
- I found no iOS overlay that translates inside WhatsApp in place. iOS restricts this, but no source was retrieved stating it.

## Which tools position themselves for language learners, and which are pure translation?

### Takeaway
Almost everything built into messengers and phone OSes is **pure translation or pure writing-fixing**, framed as "connect across languages". The products that position themselves for **learners** sit outside the user's real conversations: tutor bots you chat with (Parlai, Polyato, LangBuddy, Poly), Google Translate Practice, flashcard tools (Vocably), plus one overlay app with a wordbook and flashcards (Instant Translate On Screen). **No product found combines word-level tap on received messages in your real WhatsApp or Telegram chats, saved vocabulary with review, and explained corrections of your own outgoing message.** That combination is the proposed product's opening.

### Inferences
- **Proximity to the proposed product**, ranked roughly from closest to furthest:
  1. Instant Translate On Screen: in-chat overlay, wordbook, flashcards. It lacks word-tap in place and explained corrections before sending.
  2. Apple Writing Tools Proofread and the Grammarly keyboard: explained corrections before sending, inside any messenger. They have no vocabulary layer, and Grammarly's language support outside English is beta or limited.
  3. WhatsApp and Telegram tutor bots: learning framing, corrections and explanations. They are not in the user's real chats.
  4. The native translators: in the user's real chats, but per message and with no learning.
- **Hebrew↔Italian specifically:** among the learner-positioned products, Parlai and Polyato state both Hebrew and Italian. Among the native in-chat translators, none states Hebrew. A Hebrew-speaking learner of Italian therefore has no built-in way to get Italian messages explained *in Hebrew* inside WhatsApp or iMessage today, going by the language lists found. That is a concrete wedge for the founder's own case, but it is narrow as a market.
- **Main competitive risk:** the platforms already own translation and draft-fixing and are adding languages quickly (WhatsApp went from 6 to 19 Android packs in about 6 months, per the beta). If WhatsApp or Telegram added "tap word → definition" or "explain this correction", the third-party opening would shrink. I found no announcement of either.

### Gaps
- I found no source showing any messenger or OS planning learner features (word saving, spaced repetition, explanations of grammar) inside chats.
- I found no retention or usage data for any learner-positioned in-messenger tool, and no independent reviews comparing correction quality.
- I did not research language-exchange apps with their own chat (HelloTalk, Tandem), which have in-chat translate and correct features. They are outside this scope (they are not inside existing messengers) but are the closest functional analogue, and the report should check whether another research thread covers them.
