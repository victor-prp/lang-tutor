# Messengers translate everything, yet teach nothing

As of October 2026, no app found does the whole idea. That idea has three parts: it works inside the WhatsApp or Telegram chats a learner already has with real people; it lets them tap one word of a received message to look it up and save it for review; and it explains what is wrong with their own sentence before they send it. Every part exists somewhere, but never together. WhatsApp, Telegram, Apple, Samsung and Google now translate received messages for free, but only a whole message or a whole chat at a time, and they fix drafts without saying why. Apple's Proofread is the one documented exception. Tutor bots on WhatsApp and Telegram correct and explain, but the learner is talking to the bot, not to a friend. HelloTalk and Tandem pair translation and correction with real people, but only strangers inside their own networks. The two nearest competitors each fall short on two of the three parts. Instant Translate On Screen, an Android overlay with 5M+ installs, saves words from chats into flashcards, but you drag a bubble over the text rather than tapping a word. Tokki lets friends tap words and build a per-chat vocabulary, but only inside its own messenger, with about 300 users and no writing help. For a Hebrew speaker learning Italian the gap is wider. Italian is supported almost everywhere, but Hebrew is missing from every on-device messenger translator and every dedicated grammar checker whose language list the research found. On the technical side, no sanctioned WhatsApp interface lets a third party read a person's own chats. The clean design is an app that works alongside the messenger through the phone's own features: the text-selection menu or share sheet for received words, and a custom keyboard for drafts. Telegram is the only major network whose rules allow deep integration, but its API terms contain an AI clause that needs a ruling before anyone builds on it.

## Every messenger now translates, one whole message at a time

By late 2026, understanding a message in another language is solved for free inside every major messenger and phone operating system. That changes what a third-party app can charge for. WhatsApp began rolling out Message Translations on 23 September 2025. The user long-presses a message, taps Translate and downloads a language pack. The translation runs **on the device "where WhatsApp cannot see"** it, and Android users can turn on automatic translation for a whole chat ([About Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/09/introducing-message-translations-whatsapp/)). Android launched with six languages. A March 2026 beta added 13 packs, **Italian among them, for 19 in total, with Hebrew absent** ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-beta-for-android-adds-13-language-packs-for-message-translations/)). On iPhone, WhatsApp relies on Apple's on-device language packs. An iOS beta seen in March 2026 adds per-chat auto-translation across 21 languages, again including Italian and not Hebrew ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/20/whatsapp-for-iphone-may-soon-offer-automatic-message-translation-across-21-languages/)). Going from 6 to 19 Android packs in about six months shows how quickly a platform fills language gaps once it decides to.

Telegram translates any single message for free, and Premium users get real-time translation of whole chats. A local language-detection model decides when to offer it: only after at least eight messages, with one language detected in at least 35% of them and at least 65% of those foreign. Channels that reach a boost level can switch autotranslation on for every reader ([Telegram API](https://core.telegram.org/api/translation)). On 31 March 2026 Telegram added an **AI Editor in the message bar that "can instantly fix grammar, rewrite your text in a different style or translate your message"**. It appears once a draft passes three lines and runs on Telegram's confidential Cocoon network ([Telegram blog](https://telegram.org/blog/ai-editor-mighty-polls-and-more)). The official post does not say whether fixes are explained or whether the feature is free. One beta review reports a free cap that Premium raises roughly fiftyfold ([Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/697/telegram-ai-editor-cocoon-guide)). WhatsApp's counterpart, Writing Help, rephrases, proofreads or changes the tone of a draft using Meta's Private Processing. It **launched in English only** in August 2025 ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-launches-ai-writing-help-tool-powered-by-private-processing-on-ios-and-android/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/whatsapp-rolls-out-ai-powered-writing-help-to-rephrase-and-refine-your-words-491322-2025-08-28)), and the research found no later language list.

Phone makers add a second layer that works across apps. Apple's Live Translation in Messages (iOS 26) is turned on per contact and shows the original with the translation underneath. It needs an Apple Intelligence iPhone and a language pack of about 900 MB, and it works only inside Apple's own Messages app ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-live-translation-in-messages/)). Its launch list includes Italian and not Hebrew ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/09/new-apple-intelligence-features-are-available-today/)). Apple's Writing Tools are the **only built-in correction feature documented to explain itself**. Proofread underlines each change and lets the user step through "an explanation for each change". It works in "most places you write, including third-party apps", so it reaches WhatsApp's and Telegram's text fields ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/find-the-right-words-with-writing-tools-iph6f08da1d2/ios)).

Samsung's Writing assist translates chats inside WhatsApp, Signal, LINE and others; Telegram is not on its list. It also offers a corrected draft to Replace or Copy, with no documented explanations. It covers 10 to 16 languages depending on the regional page, Italian included and Hebrew not ([Samsung US](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10000943/); [Samsung Ireland](https://www.samsung.com/ie/support/mobile-devices/how-to-use-writing-assist)). An Israeli Samsung community thread asking for Hebrew got the reply that there is no estimate ([Samsung Members IL](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/%D7%90%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%95%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%9D/Urgent-Request-Hebrew-Support-for-Galaxy-AI-Live-Translate/td-p/21547347)). Pixel's Live Translate translates chat apps on the device in 21 languages, Italian yes and Hebrew no ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/11209263?hl=en)). Viber (long-press, powered by Google Translate), WeChat and Facebook Messenger also translate one message at a time ([Viber Help](https://help.viber.com/hc/en-us/articles/9423180947869-Chat-knowledge-base); [WebNots](https://www.webnots.com/how-to-use-wechat-translation/); [Messenger Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/messenger-app/329901696451149)).

| Built-in feature | What it translates | Draft help | Explains its fixes | Italian | Hebrew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp Translations + Writing Help | One message, or a whole chat on Android | Proofread, rephrase, tone (English at launch) | Not documented | Yes (Android beta; iOS via Apple) | Not in any list found |
| Telegram translation + AI Editor | One message free; whole chat on Premium | Fix grammar, change style, translate the draft | Not documented | Not stated | Not stated |
| Apple Live Translation + Writing Tools | Whole conversation, Apple Messages only | Proofread in any app | **Yes, for each change** | Yes | Not in any list found |
| Samsung Writing assist | Chats in WhatsApp, Signal and LINE; not Telegram | Spelling, grammar, styles | No | Yes | No |
| Pixel Live Translate | Messages inside chat apps | None | None | Yes | No |

Two patterns matter more than any single row. First, **no built-in feature lets the user tap a single word, and none saves vocabulary**. Every one of them translates a message or a whole conversation. Second, Hebrew is the weak spot of on-device translation. Server-side engines such as the Google Translate engine behind Viber almost certainly handle Hebrew, but no source in the research confirms Hebrew for Viber or Telegram. On the lists that exist, the built-in WhatsApp and iMessage translators cannot turn an Italian message into Hebrew today; they can only take it to English. The same inference applies to the learner's own app: the Apple on-device language list quoted above has no Hebrew, so an app built on Apple's Translation framework would inherit that gap and need a server-side model for Hebrew.

## Learner products help everywhere except inside real conversations

The products that present themselves as learning tools all sit outside the learner's real chats. They fall into three groups, and each puts the learner in a different place.

### Tutor bots put the learner in a chat with a machine

A crowded long tail of AI tutors lives inside WhatsApp and Telegram as a contact the learner messages. Parlai, a Vienna startup on WhatsApp, offers "real-time corrections on grammar and vocabulary", voice practice and vocabulary quizzes. It lists 31 languages including **Hebrew and Italian**, and charges €12 a month for Premium ([Parlai](https://www.parlai.app/)). Its self-reported "10k learners" sits beside **136 active paid subscriptions as of February 2026** on a revenue-verification listing ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/parlai)). That is the only hard usage number in the whole category. Polyato offers gentle corrections, games and grammar practice inside WhatsApp in 84 languages, including **Hebrew and Italian, with explanations in the learner's own language** ([Polyato](https://www.polyato.com/)). LangBuddy corrects with a one-line reason ([LangBuddy](https://langbuddy.ai/)). Zaplingo teaches English, Spanish, French and Italian over WhatsApp and Telegram ([The AI Report](https://www.theaireport.ai/tooldatabase/zaplingo)). Telegram has Poly, Telearn, Julia and Behappy Speak, the last of which remembers the learner's past mistakes ([Poly](https://bot-polyglott.net/en/); [Telearn](https://telearn.net/); [Julia](https://enjulia.com/); [Hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/behappy-speak)).

Two Telegram products come closer to a vocabulary loop. KeelAI pairs its tutor with a Telegram Mini App for spaced-repetition flashcards ([Hunted.space](https://hunted.space/dashboard/keelai)). LingoCop saves every translation to a word list and sends reminders on days 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 ([LingoCop](https://lingocop.notion.site/Learn-foreign-words-on-autopilot-with-bite-sized-practice-94bb794d70c6492f8e94807d8f6ff3e8)). Meta AI, built into WhatsApp, already works as an informal tutor: users ask it to practise a language and it comments on their level ([Crónica](https://www.cronica.com.ar/tecnologia/WhatsApp-la-manera-en-la-que-se-puede-aprender-ingles-gracias-a-Meta-AI-20240811-0174.html)). Builders already treat "correct briefly, explain if the learner asks why" as standard practice ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/elenarevicheva/building-ai-language-tutors-on-whatsapp-why-messaging-apps-beat-web-11ke)), so that interaction alone will not set a product apart. The weakness these bots share is structural. To get help with a friend's message, the learner has to copy or forward it to the bot, and then copy the bot's answer back.

### AI tutors chase voice while written correction turns free

The scaled businesses have moved toward spoken AI conversation. In Q2 2026 Duolingo reported **58.7M daily active users, 12.7M paid subscribers and $298.5M revenue**, and called Video Call its "flagship conversational practice feature", now being pushed into the cheaper Super tier ([Duolingo Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)). Speak raised money at a $1B valuation in December 2024 to teach "by talking out loud" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/10/openai-backed-speak-raises-78m-at-1b-valuation-to-help-users-learn-languages-by-talking-out-loud)), and passed $100M annualised revenue in late 2025 ([Y Combinator](https://x.com/ycombinator/status/1988738481636675978)). Reviewers report that Duolingo made Explain My Answer free in January 2026 in most courses, Italian among them. No official announcement confirms this ([StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/duolingo-max-review)). Duolingo Max courses are taught from English, and the research found no big tutor that explains Italian to Hebrew speakers in Hebrew.

Smaller apps do correct written chat inline. Loora's corrections appear inside the chat and can be tapped for detail ([Loora](https://www.loora.com/support/getting-started/what-is-loora)). Gliglish explains in either the target language or the learner's own ([DevToolLab](https://devtoollab.com/ai-tools/gliglish)). Langotalk has gone furthest toward a vocabulary loop: version 7.0 added spaced repetition and version 7.1 lets users "click key words and phrases from chats", at $19.99 a month ([App Store: Langotalk](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6449473489)). A language school's review notes that its "grammar review can sometimes be inaccurate" ([FlexiClasses](https://flexiclasses.com/langotalk-review/)). Google Translate's Practice mode (August 2025) adds tap-the-words-you-hear exercises, but it launched without Hebrew or Italian and lives inside the Translate app ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/08/26/google-translate-takes-on-duolingo-with-new-language-learning-tools/)). The conclusion is that "AI corrects your sentence" is becoming a free feature, not something a product can charge for on its own. And every one of these apps is a closed conversation with an AI.

### Exchange apps have real people, but only strangers

HelloTalk is the closest functional analogue. Long-pressing a message opens Translate, Transliteration, peer Correction (a partner edits your sentence and adds notes), AI Grammar and Favorite ([HelloTalk blog](https://www.hellotalk.com/en/blog/9-chat-tools-to-level-up-your-language-exchanges)). Its word-level lookup is disputed. A November 2025 review calls a "word-click tool, which translates a tapped word" its most practical feature ([Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/hellotalk-review/)), while HelloTalk's own pages describe translating whole messages. Either way, nothing feeds a review loop. Favorites are a tagged bookmark list, and the blog suggests copying text into "a memorization or flashcard tool". Spaced repetition is sold separately as HelloWords, one of a family of satellite AI apps on the same account ([HelloTalk AI apps](https://www.hellotalk.com/en/ai-apps)). **Free users get 10 translations a day** ([HelloTalk FAQ](https://www.hellotalk.com/ja/faq/hellotalk-basics/265)), and VIP costs $12.99 a month or $79.99 a year ([App Store: HelloTalk](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hellotalk-language-learning/id557130558)). HelloTalk claims 70M+ users ([HelloTalk About](https://www.hellotalk.com/en/about)). A third-party estimate puts its iOS app at about 679K monthly active users and more than $50K a month in in-app revenue ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/557130558)), an order of magnitude below the founder's 2023 claim of over a million paying users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HelloTalk)).

Tandem's redesigned chat adds the tools closest to "help me write my sentence". Grammar Check "fixes mistakes in your message drafts", Word Finder suggests the next word to say, and Inspire offers conversation ideas ([APKMirror: Tandem 6.29.0](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/tripod-technology-gmbh/tandem-language-exchange-speak-any-language/tandem-language-exchange-6-29-0-release/)). The release notes do not say whether Grammar Check explains its fixes. A third-party pricing page reports 3 free translations a day, with Pro at $13.99 a month ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/tandem/pricing)). Its last official member count is over ten million, from 2020 ([UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/news/language-exchange-app-tandem-raises-45-million-in-series-a-funding-led-by-brighteye-ventures-20200729)).

None of these apps imports WhatsApp or Telegram contacts, and none works inside those messengers. The complaints users make about them trace back to the stranger network itself. Users say the apps feel like dating apps ("Tandem seems to be a multilingual flirting app", [Reddit](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/languagelearning/comments/1er5o3w/deleted_by_user/lhwf83i/?context=3)). They report floods of requests, one reviewer counting 50 in under an hour, many "from people who aren't native or fluent in my target language" ([App Store: Tandem](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tandem-conversation-exchange/id959001619)), and a 15-year-old on Speaky described "unwanted nudity and love messages" ([App Store: Speaky](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1118877445)). One review notes that AI corrections arrive looking exactly like human ones ([Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/hellotalk-review/)). Two products have tried learning inside chats with friends, both as standalone messengers. In the 2015 iOS app Hiway, users could "chat with your friends, click on words, and add them to your learning deck", and its current status is unknown ([Hacking Chinese](https://challenges.hackingchinese.com/resources/stories/379-hiway-english-chinese-language-exchange-community)). Tokki, "a messenger that teaches you", lets a group pick a target language per chat. Members tap a word or two to look them up and save them to that chat's vocabulary, and saved words are then swapped into everyone's messages in the target language. Tokki is free and covers 16 languages, Italian yes and Hebrew no. It reports "300+ people" and offers no AI writing help ([Tokki](https://tokkiapp.com/)).

## Keyboards, overlays and readers each cover half the job

The tools that do reach every messenger split the task in two. A keyboard sees only what the user is typing, and an overlay sees only what is on screen.

Keyboards own outgoing text. Gboard translates as you type ([Android Ayuda](https://en.androidayuda.com/gboard-keyboard-google-integrates-translator/)), and its Proofread checks grammar and offers "Fix it", with no explanation for each error and with official support for US English on Pixel 8 only ([Gboard Help](https://support.google.com/gboard/answer/14164531?hl=en)). The Grammarly keyboard is the exception that explains: tapping its button "expands the errors with explanations for the proposed change" ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2017/12/14/grammarly-releases-android-keyboard-make-grammar-better/)). Its autocorrect is English-only ([App Store: Grammarly](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1158877342)), and **Italian arrived in beta in September 2025** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250910688436/en/Grammarly-Expands-Beyond-English-With-AI-Writing-Assistance-in-5-New-Languages)). AI keyboards such as ReplyAssistant and Keyboard Copilot check grammar, rephrase and translate before sending ([App Store: ReplyAssistant](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ai-keyboard-replyassistant/id1664247219); [Keyboard Copilot](https://www.keyboardcopilotapp.com/)). One Play Store grammar keyboard markets itself to "language learners" but describes no explanations ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.SpellCheck.EnglishCorrection.AutoSpellCheckerKeyboard)). The only keyboard found that was built to teach, WordsU, looks defunct ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/smartphone-keyboard-teaches-while-you-text)). No mainstream keyboard saves vocabulary.

The dedicated writing checkers repeat the Hebrew gap. Grammarly lists roughly 23 to 28 languages with Italian and without Hebrew, and its advanced suggestions cover only six languages, Italian among them ([Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/languages)). LanguageTool's roughly 30 languages exclude Hebrew, and its maintainers say new languages depend on community contributors ([LanguageTool](https://languagetool.org/languages); [LanguageTool forum](https://forum.languagetool.org/t/please-add-support-for-hebrew/7703)). DeepL Write supports nine languages, Italian yes and Hebrew no ([DeepL blog](https://www.deepl.com/blog/deepl-write-welcomes-french-spanish)). **No dedicated checker found explains Italian grammar in Hebrew**, so general-purpose language models are the only option today. Even they are uneven: a 2025 study found that ChatGPT's grammar explanations changed with the language of the prompt ([VNU Journal of Foreign Studies](https://jfs.ulis.vnu.edu.vn/index.php/fs/article/download/5485/4632/14680)). Explanation quality in Hebrew therefore has to be tested, not assumed.

Overlays own received text, but only on Android. Instant Translate On Screen uses accessibility and screen-capture permissions to translate inside WhatsApp, LINE, Telegram and Instagram DMs. It **already has a learner layer**: users can "save tricky vocabulary into Word Mode for spaced review, flashcards, and quizzes", plus a personal wordbook. Its floating "Magic Dot" helps the user reply in any input box. It claims 200+ languages and 5M+ installs ([Instant Translate On Screen](https://screen-translate.vercel.app/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.spaceship.screen.textcopy&hl=en)). The interaction is dragging a bubble over a region, not tapping a word where it sits, and outgoing help is translation, not explained correction. Translator for WhatsApp (LangLang) auto-translates every message through the accessibility service ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.langlang.translator_for_wsp&hl=en_IN)), and Tap To Translate Screen fills the reply box with a translated reply ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tap_to_translate.snap_translate&hl=en_US)). On desktop, DeepL and Flow Translate add translate buttons to WhatsApp Web ([DeepL Support](https://support.deepl.com/hc/en-us/articles/7786508619420-DeepL-for-WhatsApp-Web); [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/flow-translate-for-whatsa/mngnfieandhlpbbedebnokeghkfjpgkc)), and none of them saves words.

Reading tools have perfected exactly the loop the idea needs, but on media. LingQ, Language Reactor, Readlang, Toucan and Migaku all tie word-level lookup to saving and flashcards. They work on articles, imported text, Netflix and YouTube ([comprehensibleinput.it.com](https://comprehensibleinput.it.com/blog/best-comprehensible-input-apps-2026); [Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/best-chrome-extensions-for-language-learning/)). None documents support for chats. Readlang and Toucan claim to work on any web page, which in principle includes web.whatsapp.com, but nobody has tested it. Messages from people the learner knows are arguably the most personally relevant reading a learner ever gets, and they are the one kind of text this mature tooling does not reach.

## Two near-misses, and Hebrew is the open flank

Set side by side, each product meets at most two of the idea's requirements: being in your real chats, word-level lookup feeding review, and explained correction before sending.

| Product | In your existing chats with real people | Tap a word in a received message | Saves words for review | Explains your draft's mistakes | Italian | Hebrew |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instant Translate On Screen (Android) | Yes, as an overlay | Drag a bubble over a region | **Yes** (wordbook, spaced review) | No (translates replies) | 200+ languages claimed | Unknown |
| Tokki | Friends, but in its own messenger | **Yes** | **Yes** (per-chat vocabulary, quizzes) | No | Yes | No |
| Apple Writing Tools / Grammarly keyboard | Yes, any app's text field | No | No | **Yes** | Yes / beta | Not confirmed / No |
| WhatsApp, Telegram, Samsung, Pixel built-ins | **Yes** | No (whole message) | No | No (fix only) | Yes (Telegram not stated) | No (Telegram not stated) |
| HelloTalk | Strangers, its own network | Disputed | Bookmarks; spaced repetition in a separate app | Peers and AI Grammar | 260+ languages claimed | Not stated |
| Tandem | Strangers, its own network | Not documented | Not documented | Grammar Check fixes drafts | 300+ languages claimed | Not stated |
| Parlai, Polyato (WhatsApp bots) | No, you chat with a bot | Only text you forward | Quizzes (Parlai) | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| Langotalk, LingoCop | No, AI or a bot | Yes (key words) / what you send | **Yes** | AI feedback / No | Varies | Not stated |
| LingQ, Language Reactor, Readlang | No, media only | **Yes** | **Yes** | No | 30+ to 100+ languages claimed | Not stated |

Instant Translate On Screen is the product to benchmark against. It is already used over WhatsApp at scale and already turns chat text into flashcards. It lacks three things: precise word tapping, grammar explanation, and any presence on iOS. Tokki is the more instructive precedent for design. Its idea of swapping learned words back into friends' messages turns a group chat into graded input, which nothing else does. But it asks friends to move to a new messenger, and at 300 users there is no evidence that they will. Hiway's 2015 attempt and unknown fate point the same way. A reasonable but unproven inference is that standalone friend-chat apps stall because nobody's friends switch.

The Hebrew column is where the founder's own case becomes a wedge. Among learning products, only Parlai and Polyato state both Hebrew and Italian, and both are bots. Every on-device messenger translator and every dedicated grammar checker found leaves Hebrew out. So a Hebrew speaker today has no built-in way to get an Italian message explained in Hebrew inside WhatsApp or iMessage, and no checker that explains an Italian mistake in Hebrew. The audience is narrow as a market. But it is a pairing the platforms visibly underserve. A plausible inference, not something the sources state, is that their on-device approach, with a pack downloaded per language, makes Hebrew slower for them to add than for an app that translates on a server.

The main competitive risk is absorption by the platforms themselves. A single beta took WhatsApp's Android language packs from 6 to 19 in about six months, and Telegram, WhatsApp, Apple and Samsung all ship draft-fixing already. The research found no announcement of word-level lookup or explained corrections from any messenger or OS. But both are small steps from what already ships, and Apple's Proofread already takes the second one.

## WhatsApp stays sealed, Telegram opens with an AI catch

The integration question decides the product's shape more than any competitor does.

### WhatsApp offers three doors, each barred differently

The official WhatsApp Business Platform carries only messages people send to a business number, so it can never see a learner's chats with friends. Since 15 January 2026, Section 4.7 of its terms has said that providers of AI technology "are strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Platform" when AI is the primary functionality offered ([Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)). ChatGPT, Perplexity and Luzia left WhatsApp under this rule, while support and booking bots remain allowed, and **Meta decides what counts as "primary"** ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/20/whatsapp-bans-ai-chatbots-from-using-its-business-api/)). The same section also bars using platform data to train AI, except for fine-tuning a model for the business's own exclusive use.

Regulators have carved out exceptions region by region. Italy's competition authority ordered the terms suspended on 24 December 2025 ([AGCM](https://en.agcm.it/en/media/press-releases/2025/12/A576)). Brazil won an exemption, after which Meta charged rival AI chatbots **$0.0625 per non-template message** from 11 March 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/06/after-europe-whatsapp-will-let-rival-ai-companies-offer-chatbots-in-brazil/)). On 9 June 2026 the European Commission ordered interim measures restoring access, saying that a fee "is in practice equivalent to the previous access ban". The measures last until the investigation ends or June 2029, and Meta says it will appeal ([AP via News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/06/09/eu-orders-meta-to-restore-whatsapp-access-for-rival-ai-chatbots/)). One report says ChatGPT was back on WhatsApp across the EEA by July ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320553/20260715/eu-antitrust-order-reopens-whatsapp-chatgpt-setting-precedent-ai-markets.htm)). The research found no reporting after that on compliance or on the appeal. **Israel is outside every carve-out.** A WhatsApp tutor number whose product is an LLM therefore runs at Meta's discretion for a Hebrew-speaking audience. Small tutors such as Parlai still operate, which suggests enforcement is lax, but that is a risk the product carries, not a right it holds.

The second door is to act as the user's own WhatsApp client, which is how Beeper and the open-source bridges work. Unofficial libraries such as Baileys, whatsapp-web.js and whatsmeow connect as a linked device. WhatsApp's consumer terms forbid access "through automated or other means" in unauthorised ways, reverse engineering, "derivative works", and building software "that function[s] substantially the same as our Services" ([WhatsApp Terms](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/terms-of-service-eea)). The mautrix bridge's own docs warn that "WhatsApp is known to ban accounts that are too suspicious", though "just using the bridge shouldn't cause any bans" ([mautrix docs](https://docs.mau.fi/bridges/go/whatsapp/authentication.html)). Beeper, owned by Automattic, has run this way at scale without visible enforcement. It moved its bridges onto the user's device in July 2025, offers a local Desktop API that it says is for "personal use only", and has **no translation or learning feature** ([Beeper FAQ](https://beeper.com/faq); [Beeper Desktop API](https://developers.beeper.com/desktop-api/)). WhatsApp's wave of wrongful suspensions in August and September 2026, which Meta blamed on a technical error and whose listed triggers include "unusual usage patterns", shows how little protection a user of such a client has ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/03/meta-admits-technical-error-caused-wrong-whatsapp-account-suspensions-says-mcmc)).

The third door is EU messaging interoperability under the Digital Markets Act (DMA). Since November 2025, users in Europe can opt in to "third-party chats" with partner apps, which must provide the same level of end-to-end encryption (E2EE) as WhatsApp. These chats are one-to-one only, on Android and iOS only, and in the European Region only ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/messaging-interoperability-whatsapp-enables-third-party-chats-for-users-in-europe/)). A learning app could in principle become such a messenger, and then show tap-to-translate on its users' WhatsApp conversations. But it would have to build a full E2EE messenger, the friend would have to opt in, and existing history and groups would not come across. The first partner, BirdyChat, had fewer than 500 Android downloads at launch ([Mobile Ecosystem Forum](https://mobileecosystemforum.com/2025/11/17/whatsapps-first-eu-interoperability-partners-named-but-will-birdychat-and-haiket-move-the-needle/)). It is an enormous effort for a learning feature, and it excludes Israel.

### Telegram permits deep integration but restricts AI

Telegram explicitly allows third-party clients. Each needs its own api_id and may use "Telegram" in its name only when preceded by "Unofficial". It must keep basic features working and must not act "on behalf of the user without the user's knowledge and consent" ([Telegram API Terms](https://core.telegram.org/api/terms)). A learning client built on Telegram's TDLib library would see every chat and control the composer, so it could offer real word-tapping and a coached draft on both Android and iOS. The catch is **clause 1.5**. It prohibits "using, accessing or aggregating data obtained from the Telegram platform to … engage in the development, enhancement or deployment of artificial intelligence, machine learning models and similar technologies", with no stated exception for features the user starts. Sending a friend's message to an LLM for translation could be read as deployment. The research found no Telegram guidance on this, so a written ruling should come before any build.

The lighter route works inside the official Telegram app. A **Telegram Business connected bot** receives "messages from both chat participants" in the private chats the user chooses. It can reply as the user, with a small indicator, and it can message the user in its own chat ([grammY](https://grammy.dev/advanced/business)). Since Bot API 10.0 on 8 May 2026, this no longer needs Telegram Premium ([Bot API changelog](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api-changelog)). A learner could therefore have glosses and corrections for a friend's messages delivered to a bot chat or a Mini App. But the bot cannot add anything to the friend's message bubble itself, it covers private chats rather than groups, and clause 1.5 applies here too. Mini Apps on their own cannot read chats, though they can pre-fill the input field ([Telegram Mini Apps](https://core.telegram.org/bots/webapps)).

### Android offers three ways in; iOS offers only what the user selects

On Android, the cleanest route for tap-a-word is **ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT**. The app adds an entry to the text-selection menu of every app, the same mechanism Google Translate uses, and it needs no special permission ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2015/10/in-app-translations-in-android.html?hl=en)). The user long-presses a word in WhatsApp, picks "Learn with …", and gets an explanation and a save button. The open question is whether WhatsApp and Telegram let users select a single word inside a message bubble. The research could not verify this, and the route depends on it. A **Notification Listener** sees new incoming message text without the app being open, as AutoResponder for WhatsApp shows ([AutoResponder](https://www.autoresponder.ai/whatsapp)), so incoming messages could be queued for study. It does not see chat history or the user's own messages, and sideloaded apps face restricted settings ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/09/12/android-15-sideloaded-apps-restrictions/)). The **AccessibilityService**, which Instant Translate On Screen relies on, sees the whole screen. In return, Play requires a prominent in-app disclosure, a declaration with a video, and a ban on autonomous actions ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en)). Android 17's opt-in Advanced Protection Mode blocks it for apps that are not accessibility tools, according to beta reports ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/android-17-blocks-non-accessibility.html)). A custom keyboard (IME) reads and rewrites the draft in any messenger ([InputConnection](https://spot.pcc.edu/~mgoodman/developer.android.com/reference/android/view/inputmethod/InputConnection.html)).

On iOS, **no API lets a third-party app read another app's chats or notifications**, and App Review rule 2.5.2 bars apps from reading data outside their own container ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Received text reaches the app only when the user selects it and taps Share, or copies and pastes it. A custom keyboard extension can check and rewrite the draft. It sees only the text before the cursor and needs Full Access for network calls. Even so, it must still work without Full Access to pass App Review, host apps can refuse custom keyboards, and the system swaps it out in secure fields ([Apple Custom Keyboard guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/General/Conceptual/ExtensibilityPG/CustomKeyboard.html); [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). App Review rule 5.2.2 also requires permission under the third party's terms to display its content, which adds review risk to any approach that scrapes WhatsApp. Apple shut down Beeper Mini's reverse-engineered iMessage within days in December 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/14/apple-again-targets-imessage-on-android-app-beeper-but-company-claims-it-has-a-fix)). Inside Apple Messages, an iMessage app extension can insert text into the composer, but it cannot read the conversation ([Apple: MSConversation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/messages/msconversation.md)).

The other networks offer only business bots. The Messenger Platform serves business Pages, not personal accounts ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/overview)). Viber charges **€100 a month for each bot** created since February 2024 ([Viber Help](https://help.viber.com/hc/articles/15247629658525-Bot-commercial-model)). LINE bots run through Official Accounts ([LINE Developers](https://developers.line.biz/en/docs/partner-docs/development-guidelines/)). Signal has no official API, and the EU ruled that iMessage is not a gatekeeper service, so the DMA imposes no interoperability duty on it ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/02/13/apple-imessage-escapes-dma-regulation-eu/)).

| Route | Tap a word in a received message | Help with your own draft | Platforms | Terms risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Android text-selection action (ACTION_PROCESS_TEXT) | The word the user selects | None | Android | None; a standard feature, if bubbles allow selecting a word |
| iOS Share or Action extension, or copy and paste | The text the user selects | None | iOS, Android | None |
| Custom keyboard (Android IME, iOS extension) | Cannot see the chat | Reads and rewrites the draft | Both | Low; on iOS it must work without Full Access |
| Android Notification Listener | New incoming text only | Reply through the notification | Android | Low; restricted for sideloaded apps |
| Android AccessibilityService | The whole screen | Reads and types | Android | Play disclosure, declaration and suspension risk; blocked under Advanced Protection |
| Telegram Business bot | Both sides of the chats the user picks, with glosses in the bot chat | Replies as the user, marked | Both | Allowed; AI clause 1.5 unresolved |
| Telegram third-party client (TDLib) | Full | Full | Both | Allowed; AI clause 1.5 unresolved; user must switch apps |
| WhatsApp Business tutor number | Only messages the user forwards | In the bot chat, copied back | Both | AI-primary use at Meta's discretion outside Italy, Brazil and the EU |
| WhatsApp linked-device client (Beeper-style) | Full | Full | Both | Breaks WhatsApp's terms; risk of account bans |
| WhatsApp DMA interoperability | Full, in your own messenger | Full | Both, EU only | Allowed; requires a full E2EE messenger and 1:1 chats only |

## Conclusion

The research changes what the product should claim to be. It should not claim to integrate with WhatsApp. It should be a learning layer that the phone's own features carry into every messenger: the selection menu or share sheet for a word the learner did not understand, and a keyboard for the sentence they are about to send. That design is the one that is legal on both platforms. It also turns the platforms' free translation from a threat into a floor: they translate the message, and the app teaches the word. It points to Android first, where the selection menu and notification access allow word-tapping in WhatsApp without breaking its terms, while iOS stays a select-and-share experience until Apple opens more. Telegram is the right place to test a deeper version, but only after Telegram answers whether clause 1.5 covers a user asking for one word's meaning. And one cheap experiment decides most of this: open WhatsApp and Telegram on Android and iOS and check whether a single word in a received bubble can be selected. If it can, the cleanest route works; if it cannot, the design falls back to sharing the whole message and choosing the word inside the app.

The pricing lesson runs against the incumbents. HelloTalk and Tandem ration translation (10 and 3 a day) while WhatsApp, Apple and Telegram give it away. A product that charges for lookups would be charging for something the user's phone already does free. The parts nobody gives away are the learner's own record and the explanations: which words they met in real conversations, which mistakes they keep making, and why each one was wrong, in Hebrew. Those are also the parts the platforms have shown no sign of building. The Hebrew–Italian pairing is a small market, but it is precisely where on-device translators and grammar checkers stop, so it is a defensible place to start rather than a niche to grow out of.
